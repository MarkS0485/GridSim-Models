# How this model was built: ERCOT transmission 345/138 kV 2025 peak hour 2025-08-18 17:00 CDT (UnvalidatedAtScale, r3)

These models were generated by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), working with full access to GridSim's core code base. That access is what allowed generic but detailed models to exist and to be grounded in real, published data from real operators around the world. These models are not approved for anything other than basic demonstrations. They do provide an exceptional starting point for people to expand into a much more detailed system.

General-purpose fidelity only. This is a solvable approximation for one snapshot, never planning grade, and it has not been validated against official studies (hence UnvalidatedAtScale in its name). It was assembled by a private build pipeline using GridSim's solver, from published open data only, and checked with GridSim's own power flow.

##### What is kept (selection rules, version 3)

- lines: power=line|cable ways with any voltage part >= 60 kV, plus ways
  whose voltage tag cannot be read at all (counted in census.json). minor_line is left out.
- substations: power=substation|station|sub_station, nodes, ways and relations, except
  substation=minor_distribution, substation=distribution with no voltage >= 60 kV,
  those whose voltage tag reads and is all below 60 kV, and those with no readable
  voltage that no kept line comes within 1.5 km of (local distribution sites).
- plants: power=plant, tags and centre point. power=generator is left out.
- transformers: power=transformer with a voltage part >= 60 kV, tags and
  centre point (evidence that a site with no voltage tag couples two voltages).

- This is the State of Texas, not yet the ERCOT footprint. Parts of Texas are
  outside ERCOT: El Paso (WECC), the Panhandle and the north-east (SPP), and the
  south-east around Beaumont (MISO). ERCOT also has DC ties to SPP, WECC and Mexico.
  min_kv 60 keeps 345, 138 and 69 kV, all of which ERCOT treats as transmission.

## 3. How it was constructed and iterated

Method in brief: a snapshot build. The data the map needs was fetched once; the network was assembled (voltage classes, cuts at the borders, line and cable parameters from standard types, transformer units, plant placement, demand allocation, dispatch, slack); then solved with GridSim. Each round let the solver's findings (reactive shortfalls, overloads, angle spread, islands, fault levels) point at the next data gap. Rules held throughout: no value is changed just because it clears a solver symptom (a value adjusted to solve with no source is tagged synthetic and listed below); every value carries an evidence class (attested, derived, estimated, synthetic); a deeper, better-sourced round is kept even where it looks worse, because it exposes a real data gap; no round was ever overwritten.

## 4. Fitted and assumed values register

### Values adjusted to fit the solve (synthetic; NO-FITTING rule)

Each item below was chosen because an earlier build did not solve, without a cited source; the elements it touches are tagged synthetic in provenance and evidence_tags.json.

- Demand aggregated to the 138 kV bus of the nearest 345/138 kV coupling station (3178 demand rows moved; GSP-style convention, chosen because the spread allocation over every 138 kV bus had no AC solution): moved demand rows are synthetic.
- Autotransformer units raised to the demand each station serves (served MVA / (0.7 x 600 MVA), at least 2): 37 sites, units 398 -> 468: those transformers are synthetic.
- All gas plants committed and dispatched pro rata (instead of largest-first commitment), spreading gas output over the state incl. the Houston industrial CHP; argued from the peak hour but chosen after a failed solve.
- Plants of 100-500 MW placed at a 345 kV bus when one lies within 15 km (placement rule changed after a failed solve).

## 5. Known gaps

### Known gaps

- No 69 kV network: demand stands for everything below 138 kV; 138 kV fragments joined to the grid only through 69 kV are not modelled.
- Line parameters are one typical design per voltage (345 kV twin Rail, 138 kV single Drake); real ERCOT lines include bundled 1590 kcmil and ACSS CREZ circuits with much higher ratings, and older 138 kV lines with lower ones.
- Transformer impedances and sizes are typical (2 x 600 MVA, 8 %); no open ERCOT transformer list.
- No series compensation (the CREZ 345 kV lines in west Texas carry series capacitors), no SVC / STATCOM, no switched shunts: the bus shunts are fixed repairs.
- Wind and solar dispatched at one capacity factor each over the whole state; the snapshot hour's real west / south split is not used.
- Weather zones are approximated from county-to-anchor distances; ERCOT's own county list is not used.
- Fleet is EIA-860M September 2026 while the snapshot hour is 2025-08-18: newer plants run in the 2025 hour.
- DC-tie ratings are recalled, not read from an ERCOT document ([VERIFY]).
- No CEII used: impedances, ratings and topology are NOT ERCOT's planning-case values.
